# Plumbing and HVAC Estimation Rules

**Version:** 1.1 Draft — discipline review required  
**Last reviewed:** 12 November 2026

## 1. Purpose and boundary

This document defines quantity takeoff evidence for plumbing, drainage, and HVAC. It is not a substitute for hydraulic, mechanical, or code-compliance design.

## 2. Plumbing and drainage

### Required inputs
- Approved water supply, hot water, drainage, and riser layouts.
- Fixture/equipment schedules and connection details.
- Pipe material, nominal size, pressure/class, insulation, and jointing specification.
- Pipe routes, elevations, slopes, riser positions, and connection points.
- Valves, traps, cleanouts, supports, sleeves, and accessories as scheduled.
- Applicable project specifications and approved engineering design.

### Quantity rules
- Count fixtures and connection points from approved plans/schedules.
- Measure pipe by the actual documented route, separated by system, material, size, and elevation where relevant.
- Count fittings and accessories from details or a documented takeoff method.
- Do not add a generic route padding factor. Any allowance must have an explicit source and approval.
- Do not infer pipe diameter, slope, or route from room count alone.

## 3. HVAC

### Required inputs
- Approved HVAC layouts, equipment schedules, and coordination drawings.
- Duct routes, dimensions, shape, material, pressure class, and fittings.
- Pipe routes and specifications for refrigerant/condensate where applicable.
- Insulation material, thickness, and surface coverage requirements.
- Equipment, grilles, diffusers, dampers, access panels, and controls schedules.

### Quantity rules
- Measure duct runs by centerline route and section dimensions; calculate sheet-metal surface area using a documented fitting method.
- Measure insulation against the specified surfaces and thickness; do not confuse insulation area with insulation volume.
- Measure refrigerant and condensate pipe by documented route and specified sizes.
- Count equipment and terminals from approved schedules.
- Do not add generic route allowances or infer equipment capacities.

## 4. Validation and status

- Reconcile plan, section, riser, and schedule references.
- Flag missing elevations, routes, dimensions, specifications, and unresolved coordination.
- Separate each service/system and size.
- Mark incomplete or conflicting takeoffs Tentative or Needs Review.
- Design-dependent decisions require review by the relevant qualified discipline engineer.

## 5. Non-goals

The quantity engine does not design pipe networks, size ducts, select equipment, calculate loads, or certify code compliance.
